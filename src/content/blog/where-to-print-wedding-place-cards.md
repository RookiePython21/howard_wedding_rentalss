# Where to Print Wedding Place Cards (Without the Hassle)

Take a deep breath. If you have already designed your place cards and you are just trying to figure out where to actually print them, you are closer to done than it feels.

Let's cut through the noise. There are really only a few real options: a local print shop, a drugstore photo counter, a home printer, or ordering them custom-made. Here is what each one actually involves.

---

## Option 1: Local Print Shops

Most towns have at least one local print shop that handles stationery jobs — the kind of place that also prints business cards and invitations.

**How it works:** You bring in your design file (or they help you build one), choose your paper stock, and they print and cut the cards for you.

**The benefit:** A real person checks your file before it prints, so you catch spelling errors or formatting issues before a hundred cards come out wrong.

**The tradeoff:** Turnaround depends entirely on the shop's schedule. Call ahead and ask about lead time, especially close to your wedding date.

**Your action:** If you go this route, call first. Ask what file format they need and how many business days they need before pickup.

---

## Option 2: Drugstore and Big-Box Photo Counters

Stores with a photo printing counter can sometimes print place cards, especially if your design is simple — just a name on a card, nothing elaborate.

**How it works:** You upload your design through their photo app or website, select a card or cardstock size, and pick up the printed sheets in-store or have them shipped.

**The benefit:** It is usually the cheapest option and often same-day if you print in-store.

**The tradeoff:** These counters are built for photos, not stationery. Fine card stock, specialty cutting, and precise color matching are not their strength. Fonts can render differently than they looked on your screen, so always ask for a proof or order a small test batch first.

**Your action:** If your design is simple text on a plain card, this can work. If you have a detailed design with color accents or a specific font, test print one sheet before committing to the full batch.

---

## Option 3: Printing at Home

If you own a decent printer and found free place card templates online, printing at home is the lowest-cost option.

**How it works:** Download or design your template, load perforated place card sheets (sold at most craft and office stores), and print.

**The benefit:** Total control over timing. No waiting on a shop's schedule.

**The tradeoff:** This is the option most likely to eat your evening. Perforated sheets jam, ink runs low mid-batch, and cutting by hand (if you are not using perforated stock) takes longer than people expect. For 100+ cards, this adds up to real hours.

**Your action:** If you choose this route, print a full test sheet first and let it sit for a few minutes to check the ink does not smear, before running the whole batch.

## Mistakes That Cost People a Reprint

A few things trip brides up no matter which printing option they choose.

**Printing before the guest list is final.** This is the single biggest cause of reprints. Even one or two late RSVPs can mean redoing a whole batch if you printed too early.

**Not checking for name consistency.** If your invitation list says "Katherine" but your RSVP tracker says "Kate," pick one and use it everywhere. Mismatched names across your wedding materials look sloppy even when the mistake is tiny.

**Skipping the proof.** Whether you are printing at home, at a drugstore counter, or through a shop, always look at one printed card in hand before approving the full run. A font that looked fine on a screen can look cramped or oddly spaced on paper.

**Underestimating how long cutting takes.** If your cards are not pre-perforated, cutting a hundred-plus cards by hand with scissors or a paper cutter takes longer than people plan for. Build in an evening for it, not an hour.

**Forgetting a few blanks.** Plan on printing five to ten extra blank cards. Plus-ones change, names get misspelled, and having a blank on hand means a fix takes minutes instead of a whole reprint.

## Where to Print Wedding Place Cards: How to Decide

The good news? This decision is simpler than it looks. Ask yourself one question: do you want to manage the printing, or hand it off?

- **Want full creative control and have the time?** A local print shop or home printing works, as long as you build in a buffer for proofing and reprints.
- **Need something fast and simple-looking?** A drugstore counter can work for plain text designs — just test print first.
- **Want it off your plate entirely?** Custom-printed place cards mean you send the names and get back finished cards.

There is no wrong answer here. Pick the option that matches how much of this particular task you actually want to do yourself.

---

## What About Paper and Card Stock?

The option you choose for printing often decides your card stock options too, and that is worth knowing upfront.

**Local print shops** usually carry a range of weights and finishes — matte, linen, pearl, and heavier cardstock that feels substantial when a guest picks it up. If texture and feel matter to you, this is where you will find the most choice.

**Drugstore photo counters** tend to offer one or two stock options at most, usually a standard glossy or matte photo paper. It works, but it will not feel like dedicated stationery.

**Home printing** depends entirely on what you buy. Craft stores sell perforated place card sheets in several weights, so you have some control, but your home printer's ink quality sets the ceiling on how sharp the final print looks.

**Custom-printed place cards** come on stock chosen for stationery use, so you are not guessing whether a particular paper will run well through a given printer.

None of these is "wrong." It just helps to know that the printing method and the paper quality are tied together, so pick based on how much the physical card matters to you, not just the design on the screen.

## What to Have Ready Before You Print (Anywhere)

Wherever you print, a few things make the process smoother:

- **A finalized guest list.** Printing cards before RSVPs settle means reprinting later.
- **Correct name spelling and formatting.** Double-check nicknames, hyphenated last names, and plus-ones before anything goes to print.
- **Table assignments decided.** If your cards include a table number, that number needs to be locked in first. For how meal choices sometimes factor into that, see our guide on [place cards with meal choice](https://www.howardweddingrentals.com/blog/wedding-place-cards-meal-choice).
- **A plan for display.** Deciding how place cards will be arranged at the reception — alphabetically, by table, on a table or an escort card display — shapes how you should order and sort them before printing.

## FAQ

**How far in advance should I print my place cards?**
Aim to print about two to three weeks before the wedding, after RSVPs are in but with enough buffer for a reprint if something needs fixing.

**Can I print place cards and escort cards the same way?**
Usually yes — the printing process is the same. The difference is just what is written on the card and how it is displayed. Our guide on [escort cards](https://www.howardweddingrentals.com/blog/wedding-escort-cards-guide) breaks down that distinction.

**What if a guest's name changes after I print?**
It happens. Keep a few blank matching cards on hand so you can hand-write a late addition without it looking out of place.
