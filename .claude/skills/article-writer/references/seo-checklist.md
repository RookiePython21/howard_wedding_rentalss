# SEO Checklist — articleWriter

On-page SEO requirements for every post. The goal is to rank *without* sounding like a robot —
so when an SEO "best practice" fights the brand voice, the voice wins on phrasing and SEO wins
on structure. They rarely actually conflict: short paragraphs and clear headers are good for both.

---

## The metadata package (deliver at the top of every final article)
- **SEO title tag** — ≤ 60 characters, includes the target keyword near the front, reads like a human wrote it. Not a banned phrase.
- **Meta description** — ≤ 155 characters, includes the keyword once, has a calm hook and a soft reason to click.
- **URL slug** — lowercase, hyphenated, 3–6 words, keyword-forward (e.g. `wedding-seating-chart-tips`).

---

## On-page structure
- **One H1** — the article title. Keyword appears naturally.
- **Keyword in the first 100 words**, worked in conversationally — never forced.
- **H2s for every major section; H3s for sub-points.** Scannable beats dense.
- **Short paragraphs** (1–4 sentences). This matches the brand voice perfectly.
- **Keyword + close variants** spread naturally through the body. No stuffing. If a sentence sounds awkward because of a keyword, rewrite the sentence — readability outranks density.
- **Suggest 1–3 images** with descriptive, keyword-aware **alt text** (as bracketed notes like `[IMAGE: pews arranged for an outdoor ceremony — alt: "wooden church pews set up for an outdoor wedding ceremony"]`). You're suggesting, not generating.
- **Internal links:** follow `internal-links.md` (3–5, natural anchors).
- **External links:** 0–2 authoritative, non-competitor sources, optional.
- **A short FAQ section** (2–4 Q&As) near the end when it fits the topic — it's bride-friendly *and* good for featured snippets. Skip it if it feels bolted-on.
- **A calm closing CTA** tied to the most relevant page.

---

## Word count
- Default **1,500–2,000 words** unless the brief says otherwise. Hit the range with genuine substance, not padding. If the topic is thin, say so in the brief stage rather than inflating it.

---

## SEO clichés that collide with the brand voice (avoid)
A lot of stock "SEO copy" is exactly what this brand bans. Watch for and avoid:
- Urgency manufacturing ("Don't wait!", "The clock is ticking", "Act now").
- The word **"perfect"** (it's banned — it triggers anxiety). Use "stunning," "calm," "the day you want."
- Corporate connectors ("Furthermore," "Moreover," "In addition").
- Keyword-stuffed, robotic sentences.

The test for any sentence: would a calm best friend who happens to know weddings actually say it out loud?
