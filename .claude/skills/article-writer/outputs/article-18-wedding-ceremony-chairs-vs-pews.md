# Wedding Ceremony Chairs vs. Pews: How to Choose

Take a deep breath. Choosing wedding ceremony chairs sounds like a small decision. Then you open a rental catalog and see six styles, four colors, and a dozen cushion options.

Here is the truth: your guests will sit in these seats for well under an hour. But the rows are the biggest shape in every ceremony photo. So the choice matters more for the *look* than for anything else.

Let's cut through the noise. This guide compares the common ceremony chair styles with solid wooden pews on three things only: how they look, how they feel to sit in, and how much work they are on the day. Then you pick one and move on.

---

## The Three Questions That Settle It

Before you compare a single chair, answer these. Write the answers down.

- **What feeling do you want?** Garden party, rustic barn, modern and clean, or a classic church feel?
- **Where is the ceremony?** A venue with its own seating, a lawn, a field, a barn floor?
- **Who is doing the setup?** The venue, a rental company, or your family the morning of?

That's it. Most couples find one style drops out of the running with each answer.

The benefit: you stop comparing everything to everything. You only compare the two or three styles that fit your answers.

## The Common Wedding Ceremony Chair Styles

Here is a quick, honest look at the chairs you'll see most often. No style is wrong. Each one just suits a different kind of day.

### White folding chairs

The default at many outdoor ceremonies. Simple and familiar. They fit almost anywhere and line up in clean rows.

The catch: a lawn full of folding chairs can read more "family reunion" than "wedding." They're light, so they shift on uneven grass and can tip in a breeze. And every guest needs their own, so a 90-guest ceremony means about 90 separate pieces to carry, set, straighten, and fold away.

### White padded folding chairs

A step up in comfort, with a cushioned seat. They look a little more finished in photos. The setup work is the same as plain folding chairs: one chair per guest, each one placed by hand.

### Chiavari chairs

The slim, elegant chairs you see at many receptions. They come in gold, silver, white, clear, and natural wood. They look polished and formal. They usually need a seat cushion for comfort, and they're best on a firm, level floor.

### Cross-back chairs

Wooden chairs with an X across the back. They suit rustic and farm weddings, and they look warm in photos. Like the others, it's one per guest.

### Wooden benches

Simple backless benches. They seat several guests each and give a casual, rustic feel. Without a back, they're less comfortable for older guests or a longer ceremony.

## Where Church Pews Fit In

Solid hardwood church pews are the other option. They're the long wooden benches with backs that you'd find in a chapel.

Here is how they compare on our three questions.

### The look

This is where pews stand apart. Chairs make a crowd of separate seats. Pews make *rows*. A handful of long wooden rows frames a real aisle, the way a church does, even in an open field with sky overhead.

Pews also bring warmth before you add a single flower. Many couples find they can cut their aisle decor down because the wood is already doing the work. Our [wedding aisle ideas post](https://www.howardweddingrentals.com/blog/wedding-aisle-ideas) shows how much the seating shapes the walk.

### The comfort

Pews have a full back, so guests can sit back and relax. Six to seven adults share each one, which keeps families together naturally. There are no gaps between seats, so nobody ends up stranded in a half-empty row.

One honest note: pews don't have individual cushions. For a typical ceremony, that's rarely a concern. If you're planning a long religious service, ask about it when you call.

### The setup

This is the part people underestimate.

One pew seats about six to seven adults. So a 90-guest ceremony needs about 14 pews instead of about 90 chairs. That's far fewer pieces to place and straighten.

Pews are heavy. That's a plus on the day, because they stay put on grass and in wind. It's also why you don't want your family moving them. ## Chairs vs. Pews at a Glance

Here is the whole comparison in one place.

- **Folding chairs:** simple and casual. Full back. About 90 pieces for 90 guests. Can shift or tip on grass. Frames the aisle loosely.
- **Chiavari or cross-back chairs:** polished or rustic. Full back. About 90 pieces for 90 guests. Happiest on a firm floor. Frames the aisle loosely.
- **Wooden benches:** casual and rustic. No back. Fewer pieces. Steady. Frames the aisle somewhat.
- **Church pews:** classic church feel. Full back. About 14 pieces for 90 guests. Very steady outdoors. Frames the aisle clearly.

## Which Style Fits Which Wedding

Still unsure? Match the seating to the kind of day you're planning.

### A backyard or family-land wedding

Your biggest worry is probably that the yard won't look like a "real" wedding. Pews answer that directly. Rows of wood make a lawn feel like a ceremony. Our [backyard wedding ideas guide](https://www.howardweddingrentals.com/blog/backyard-wedding-ceremony-ideas) covers the rest of the layout.

### An outdoor wedding where you wanted a church

If you chose outdoors but miss the church feel, pews bring it back. You get the open sky and the chapel rows at the same time.

### A modern or formal venue

Chiavari chairs suit a polished ballroom or a sleek modern space. They also match a lot of reception seating, if you want the two to look alike.

### A casual garden party

White folding chairs or simple benches fit a relaxed, come-as-you-are feel. They're easy to find and easy to understand.

### A venue that already has seating

Check first. If the venue provides chairs, you may only need to decide whether you want to swap them for a different look.

## A Few Mix-and-Match Ideas

You don't have to pick only one style. A few couples mix them on purpose.

- **Pews for the guests, chairs for the front.** A pair of special chairs for grandparents at the end of the first row.
- **Pews for the ceremony, chiavari for the reception.** Two different feelings for two parts of the day.
- **A short bench for the wedding party** if they'll sit during the ceremony.

Limiting your choices saves massive mental energy, though. If mixing feels like one more decision, skip it.

## FAQ: Wedding Ceremony Chairs

**What chairs are best for a wedding ceremony?**
It depends on the look you want. Folding chairs are simple. Chiavari chairs look polished. Cross-back chairs feel rustic. Wooden church pews give a classic church feel and frame a clear aisle.

**Are pews more comfortable than chairs?**
Pews have a full back and let families sit together. Padded chairs add a cushion. For a typical ceremony, guests are comfortable in either.

**How many pews do I need instead of chairs?**
One pew seats about six to seven adults. Divide your guest count by six to get a rough number of pews. About 14 pews seat about 84 to 98 guests.

**Can pews be used outdoors?**
Yes. Solid hardwood pews sit well on level grass and stay steady in a breeze. Tell your rental company about any slope or soft ground ahead of time.
