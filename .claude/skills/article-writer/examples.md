# Reference: Brand Tone of Voice Guide

**Audience:** Brides-to-be  
**Core Objective:** De-stress the reader and drive immediate, confident action.  
**Core Archetype:** The Calming Guide & Empowering Best Friend.

---

## 1. Core Tone Principles
*   **Empathy First:** Acknowledge the chaotic reality of wedding planning without amplifying the anxiety.
*   **Radical Clarity:** Cut through the industry noise. Use short, punchy sentences under 10 words whenever possible to induce calm.
*   **Action-Driven Structure:** Never present a problem or idea without a clear, immediate micro-step the reader can take.

---

## 2. Voice Examples (5-10 Sentences)
Use these exact sentences as a stylistic benchmark for rhythm, sentence length, and attitude:
*   Take a deep breath.
*   Your wedding is a celebration, not a test.
*   Let's cut through the noise together.
*   Pick just one task to complete today.
*   Stop letting catering quotes mix with real life.
*   Limiting your choices saves massive mental energy.
*   Pick one, book them, and move on.
*   You can design a stunning day without the panic.

---

## 3. Approved Phrases (Use Frequently)
Incorporate these phrases to maintain a supportive, momentum-building rhythm:
*   *Take a deep breath.* (Resets user focus)
*   *Let's cut through the noise.* (Signals simplicity)
*   *The benefit:* (Clearly states the value of an action)
*   *Your action:* (Directs the reader on exactly what to do next)
*   *Decision fatigue.* (Validates their mental exhaustion)
*   *Put it on a diet.* (Casual metaphor for reduction)
*   *Keep your sanity intact.* (Focuses on mental health)

---

## 4. Banned Phrases (Never Use)
Avoid corporate stiffness, overly formal transitions, or language that creates additional pressure:
*   *In order to maximize efficiency...* (Too corporate)
*   *It is imperative that you...* (Too aggressive and stressful)
*   *Furthermore / Moreover / In addition...* (Too academic)
*   *You must perfect your special day.* (Avoid the word "perfect"—it triggers anxiety)
*   *The clock is ticking.* (Creates artificial, stressful urgency)
*   *As previously mentioned...* (Stuffy and repetitive)

---

## 5. Preferred Transitions
Keep paragraphs brief and use conversational, forward-moving transitions to maintain reading momentum:
*   *But remember:*
*   *Here is the truth:*
*   *Let's fix that.*
*   *The good news?*
*   *Here is how:*
*   *Now, let's look at...*

---

## 6. Industry-Specific Terminology Preferences
Navigate the wedding industry landscape using grounded, real-world framing rather than luxury jargon:
*   **Say** *Vendor category* **instead of** *Wedding industry professional sector*.
*   **Say** *Wedding-only email inbox* **instead of** *Dedicated digital communication channel*.
*   **Say** *Top three priorities* **instead of** *Core brand alignments*.
*   **Say** *Planning paralysis* **instead of** *Inability to finalize procurement decisions*.
